# Worked example - personalised advertising watch list

## Persona

Priya, **DTC media buyer** at an eight-figure ecommerce brand. Buys on **Meta + Google**. Time budget: **60 min/week**. Mediums: text + audio. Segment: B2C/DTC. Breadth. Incentive tolerance: keep everything, flagged. Listens to podcasts at the gym, so podcast attention cost is halved.

The gym halved the podcast's attention cost and promoted it into the weekly core ahead of a second Google blog; the official release notes held their slot on coverage, not on ratio.

## Negative example (do not deliver this)

**"Top Meta creative sources for Priya (60 min/week)"** - Foxwell Digital, Jon Loomer, Dara Denney, Savannah Sanchez, sarahlevinger.com, Ben Heath (benheath.com), Limited Supply, Motion blog, Triple Whale blog. _"All the top DTC creative minds in one list - start with Sarah and Ben."_

Why it fails:

- **A bought channel is missing entirely**: Priya spends on Google every day and the list carries zero Google sources - no official release notes, no PPC news. A missed Google change costs her money directly. This alone makes it a failed deliverable, not a partial one.
- **A parked domain shipped as a link**: sarahlevinger.com is for sale. The person may well still publish - but linking a dead personal domain sends the reader to a registrar page.
- **A name-collision link shipped**: benheath.com serves an unrelated furniture-making business; the Meta-ads educator lives at heathmedia.co.uk. The URL resolves, the name matches, and it is still the wrong site - content was never checked.
- **Unverified entries presented flat**: Motion and Triple Whale were unverified at check; nothing is labelled.
- **No selling flags**: two agencies, a course seller, and two tool vendors presented as neutral voices.
